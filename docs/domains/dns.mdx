---
title: "DNS and nameservers"
description: "Manage DNS records on Pxxl, or keep your current DNS provider."
---

A domain can use **Pxxl DNS** or **external DNS**.

| Setup | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **Pxxl DNS** | Point your nameservers at Pxxl. Manage every record in the **DNS** tab. |
| **External DNS** | Keep your provider. Add the record Pxxl shows you at that provider. Pxxl can read the live values but can't edit them. |

## Switch to Pxxl DNS

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the nameservers">
    Open the domain, then **Nameservers**. Copy the Pxxl values.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Update your registrar">
    Replace the current nameservers at your registrar with the Pxxl ones.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Wait for the change to spread, then select **Verify Setup**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Recreate any records you still need (email, verification) in the **DNS** tab before or right after the switch.

## Add a record

Open **DNS**, choose **New Record**, then enter the name, type, value, and TTL.

| Type | Common use |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | Point a name at an IPv4 address. |
| **AAAA** | Point a name at an IPv6 address. |
| **CNAME** | Point a name at another hostname. |
| **TXT** | Verification, SPF, DKIM, DMARC. |
| **MX** | Email. Needs a priority from 0 to 65535. |
| **NS** | Delegate a subdomain to other nameservers. |

- For a subdomain, enter only the label, such as `api`.
- Leave TTL on **Auto** unless you need a value. Custom TTL is 60 to 86400 seconds.
- The DNS changelog keeps every change and lets you roll back.

## Example: email with Pxxl DNS

```text
Type  Name  Value                      Priority
MX    @     mx1.mailprovider.com       10
MX    @     mx2.mailprovider.com       20
TXT   @     v=spf1 include:mailprovider.com ~all
```

Use the exact values your email provider gives you.
